Practice — how it plays out for real

● ~11 min read ✎ best practices · anti-patterns · checklist

Where you'll meet it

In a SaaS product's "ask our docs" or "search our catalog" feature, embeddings sit between user queries and the corpus — behind the API tier, in front of a vector store. When latency spikes or answers go sideways, this layer gets blamed second (after the LLM) and found guilty first. In an enterprise RAG pipeline, embeddings power retrieval — and retrieval, not generation, is where 70%+ of production RAG failures start.

Docs / catalog search

Behind the API tier, in front of a vector store, feeding an LLM downstream.

Recommendation & dedup

A dense vector per entity plus a k-NN query, replacing hand-built features.

Code & multimodal search

The only practical way to query unstructured content by intent, not tokens.

Best practices

1

Pick the model for your domain, not the overall leaderboard

Do

Shortlist 3–5 MTEB models for your task, then test on a 200–500-query holdout from your own data.

Why

The top overall model averages 50+ tasks. A third-place general model often beats #1 by 5–10 points on a narrow domain.

Avoid

Using text-embedding-3-large "because the tutorial did" — it has cost real teams six-figure bills they didn't need.

2

Chunk by structure first, tokens second

Do

Split on headings, sections, or function boundaries, then cap at 256–512 tokens with 10–20% overlap.

Why

Structural chunking splitting long text into smaller pieces before embedding measured 87% retrieval accuracy vs. ~13% for fixed-size chunks on clinical data.

Avoid

1000-character chunks with 200-character overlap, applied blindly to PDFs, code, and chat logs alike.

3

Run hybrid search, then rerank

Do

Retrieve top-50 with dense + BM25 (reciprocal rank fusion), then rerank to top-5 with a cross-encoder.

Why

Dense models miss rare entities and part numbers that BM25 catches. Reranking lifts RAGAS scores 15–30% for ~50–150 ms.

Avoid

"Pure dense is enough" — invisible until someone searches "Form 1099-MISC" and gets a generic tax-tips page back.

4

Normalize once, at write time

Do

L2-normalize before storage, use dot product as the metric, and spot-check the vector norm monthly.

Why

Un-normalized vectors plus dot product silently rank by magnitude, not meaning — one of the top three dense-retrieval bugs.

Avoid

Mixing normalized and un-normalized writes because one ingestion job skipped the step.

5

Right-size dimensions; default to a Matryoshka-truncatable model

Do

Start at d=768 or 1024. If storage gets tight, truncate a Matryoshka model to 256 or 512 and re-check recall.

Why

text-embedding-3-large at d=256 beats ada-002 at d=1536 on MTEB — halving d halves your vector-DB bill.

Avoid

Picking d=3072 because "more sounds better," then quadrupling RAM at 50M vectors.

6

Match the query/passage prefix the model expects

Do

Read the model card. For e5, bge, nomic, prepend query: to queries and passage: to documents.

Why

Forgetting the asymmetric prefix silently costs 30–50% recall — the pipeline still looks like it's working.

Avoid

Copy-pasting an OpenAI call and swapping in bge-large without reading its instructions.

7

Tune the index for your actual workload

Do

For HNSW start at M=16, ef_construction=200, ef_search=64, then sweep. Past 10M vectors with tight RAM, switch to IVF-PQ.

Why

Default knobs target generic benchmarks. Filters that reject over 90% of vectors can fragment an HNSW graph and blow up tail latency.

Avoid

Treating the vector DB as a black box — "it worked at 100k in staging" is not a load test for 50M.

8

Pick the vector store for operational fit, not the leaderboard

Do

pgvector on existing Postgres under ~10–50M vectors; Qdrant for heavy filtering; Milvus at billion scale; Pinecone for zero ops.

Why

The biggest hidden cost is a second backup story and a second on-call rotation — not query latency.

Avoid

Pinecone for a 200k-vector prototype at $70/month when one CREATE EXTENSION vector would do.

9

Plan for re-embedding from day one

Do

Store the source text, chunk boundaries, model name, and model version next to every vector.

Why

Every embedding pipeline eventually switches models; mixing two model versions in one index produces noise, not results.

Avoid

"We'll re-embed when we need to" — without provenance metadata you can't even tell which vectors are stale.

10

Evaluate with a golden set, not vibes

Do

Keep a 200–1000-row set of (query, expected doc) pairs from real logs. Measure recall@10, MRR, and NDCG@10 on every change.

Why

Embedding quality drifts silently — no errors, no stack traces, just a customer complaint three weeks later.

Avoid

Trusting MTEB scores alone. Public benchmarks never replace a measurement on your own corpus.

11

Cache aggressively, keyed on (text hash, model, version)

Do

Hash the normalized text plus model id and version; store the vector in a KV store with a long TTL.

Why

Up to 60–80% of production queries are near-duplicates of recent ones. Caching cuts cost and latency together.

Avoid

Caching by raw text only — a model version bump then silently serves stale vectors.

12

Guard against leakage in evaluation

Do

Split eval sets by document or by time — never by chunk.

Why

Chunk-level splits leak near-duplicate passages across train and test, inflating recall by 10–30 points.

Avoid

Random row-level shuffles on a chunk table — the model ends up memorizing, not generalizing.

Anti-patterns to recognize

✕

Chunk-and-pray

Splitting every document into 1000-character chunks with no regard for structure, then hoping retrieval works. Fails on tables, code, and multi-sentence reasoning.

✕

Mixing model versions in one index

Re-embedding half the corpus with a new model and querying across both spaces. The spaces are unrelated; results are noise. Always full-backfill behind a feature flag.

✕

Cosine on un-normalized vectors

Storing raw outputs, querying with dot product, and assuming it equals cosine. It silently ranks by magnitude. Normalize at write time, period.

✕

Choosing the vector DB by leaderboard QPS

Picking Milvus because a benchmark blog said so, with 200k vectors and one engineer. The operational overhead dwarfs the performance gain. Match the tool to your scale.

✕

No reranker

Trusting the bi-encoder's top-5 directly. The top-50 is noisy; a cross-encoder rerank turns noise into precision. Add it before tuning anything else.

✕

Treating chunks as the source of truth

Storing only chunk text, not the parent document ID or offsets. When you need to re-chunk — and you will — you can't.

✕

Skipping the query/passage prefix

Using e5, bge, or nomic without the model's required asymmetric prefix. Halves recall, no error message, ships to prod undetected.

✕

Embedding the entire 10 MB PDF

Passing whole documents straight to an embedding endpoint, hitting context limits, and getting silently truncated. Chunk first, embed second, log token counts.

Real-world usage patterns

Mid-size SaaS support KB

~500k articles + tickets, pgvector, BGE-large at d=1024, hybrid search + Cohere rerank, P99 ~300 ms. Switching to heading-aware chunking moved NDCG@10 from 0.61 to 0.78 — bigger than any model swap.

Multilingual e-commerce

~20M products, 12 languages, Cohere embed-multilingual-v4 at d=1024 in Qdrant. Pre-filtering in_stock=true at the store level cut wasted retrieval work by 70% — after switching from HNSW to IVF.

Monorepo code search

~30M code chunks, function-level chunking, voyage-code-3, Qdrant. Function-level chunking beat file-level by ~20 NDCG points, but only once parent-class signatures were prepended to each chunk.

RAG legal assistant

~5M case-law passages, domain-tuned model, Milvus at d=768, BM25-first hybrid. BM25 outranks dense for ~40% of queries (statute numbers, party names).

Operational checklist

✓

Before you call it done

  • Tracking embed-API P50/P99 latency, vector-DB query P50/P99, weekly recall@10 on a golden set, and cache hit rate?
  • If the embedding API goes down, does the pipeline fall back to BM25-only, or fail hard — and is that tested?
  • Does every stored vector carry (model_name, model_version, embedded_at), queryable for staleness?
  • Is there a tested backfill job that re-embeds the corpus behind a feature flag with no downtime?
  • Is PII stripped before it reaches a third-party embedding API? Are vectors themselves treated as sensitive?
  • Is there a per-tenant cap on embed-API spend, a cache hit rate above 50%, and a measured reason for your d?
  • For your top 10 query shapes, what fraction of vectors survive metadata filters? Over 90% filtered means IVF, not HNSW.
  • Can a new engineer find the current model, the eval set, and how to run an offline benchmark in under 30 minutes?
  • Is a scheduled job comparing recall@10 today vs. 30 days ago? Drift is silent without it.

How it evolves in a codebase

  1. Tutorial stack. OpenAI text-embedding-3-small + Chroma or pgvector + a chunker that splits on 1000 characters. Fine for the demo and the first ~50k documents.
  2. First wave. Retrieval quality plateaus; a customer escalation forces a reranker, then hybrid search, then structural chunking — each a real refactor.
  3. Second wave. The corpus crosses ~5M vectors. Index builds take hours, or the vector-DB bill becomes visible to the CFO — triggering a store migration nobody planned for, because no one wrote down the model version per vector.
  4. Third wave. The team switches embedding models for cost, quality, or domain fit, and finally builds the re-embedding pipeline it should have built on day one.
  5. Mature stack. Structural chunking with model-version metadata, hybrid retrieval with a tuned rerank, a store sized to the corpus, a golden eval set wired into CI, and a cache layer in front.
